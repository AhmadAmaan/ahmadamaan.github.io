PROJECT 05 / FOOTBALL FORECASTING · SIMULATION

World Cup Predictor

A transparent football forecasting model combining Elo-style ratings, attack and defence profiles, squad information, and tournament rules. I ran 100,000 tournament simulations to estimate each team’s chances of progressing and winning, then explained the approach and its limitations in a Substack article.

Explore the code ↗

The problem

As a CR7 fan, and with 2026 potentially being his last World Cup, I wanted to explore what it would take for Portugal to win. A simulator makes the assumptions inspectable and shows the range of possible tournament paths.

Inside the build

  • Combine several views of team strength. Elo-style ratings, recency-weighted attack and defence profiles, opponent adjustments, and squad information contribute to the match model.
  • Simulate the tournament structure. The model includes group ranking, third-place qualification, and knockout progression. Repeated runs produce stage probabilities rather than one deterministic winner.
  • Make the analysis reproducible. CSV inputs, a configurable seed, backtesting commands, and generated charts support inspection of the model. I documented a 100,000-run experiment in the accompanying Substack article.

Scope & trade-offs

The published results are a June 2026 experiment, not current forecasts. Injury feeds and bookmaker odds were not populated in that snapshot; player coverage is uneven and projected lineups are partly heuristic. More runs reduce simulation noise, not modelling error.

What I’d explore next

Improve lineup and injury inputs, populate market-odds calibration, and evaluate calibration across more historical tournaments.
